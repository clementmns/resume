\textcolor{colorBody}{I am writing to apply for a \ifdefined\researchVariant research internship in distributed systems\else software engineering internship\fi{} with your team, between May and October 2027. \textcolor{colorBody}{At R3mScore, I build and ship features that customers use the same week. I designed and delivered a Word-to-survey import and export engine on my own, from requirements to production. It parses .docx questionnaires into structured surveys with conditional logic, loops and adaptive questions, and it replaces up to three hours of manual setup per survey. I also migrated a 15,000-line frontend from JavaScript to TypeScript incrementally, with no feature freeze and no regression in production, and added JUnit and Vitest coverage on the critical paths. Working this way taught me to keep a codebase releasable at every commit and to know which parts of a system I have not yet proven correct.}

\textcolor{colorBody}{My projects are where I go after the problems a product codebase does not hand me. As tech lead on a team of four, I architected an eight-service distributed platform with gRPC and RabbitMQ messaging, one database per service behind an Nginx gateway, and distributed tracing through OpenTelemetry. I wanted to see where a distributed system actually breaks: partial failure, retries, and the gap between what a trace shows and what a log shows. \ifdefined\researchVariant On a team of five, I also implemented Tabu Search and multi-start Simulated Annealing for a constrained Traveling Salesman Problem, and built the statistical framework comparing our algorithms from 5 to 3,000 nodes over multiple random seeds. It is where I learned that a result only counts once it survives a change of seed and of scale.\else I also built a concurrent backup engine in C\#, with parallel job execution, large-file throttling and safe access to shared state. It is where I learned that concurrency is a design problem long before it becomes a debugging problem.\fi}
